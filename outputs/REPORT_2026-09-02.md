# Rule-based skill planner vs E2E on the eurekaverse benchmark — status

All numbers are the **legged_eval protocol**, terrain seed 1, 200 cells (20 courses ×
10 difficulties), one 20 s episode per cell, upstream's goal rule (within 0.20 m held for
0.10 s, eight goals in order), equal weight over cells.  Rule-Planner runs on **depth**;
"optimistic" rows are the perception control.

---

## 1. Where everything stands

| arm | score / 8 | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **E2E teacher** | **4.83** | the upper bound.  seed 3 gives 4.98 |
| **ORACLE** — best single skill per cell | **0.81** | what a *perfect* chooser gets from this library |
| **WALK fixed + roll couple** † | **0.82** | mean of 3 seeds; the level-2 result |
| WALK fixed † | 0.76 | the lower bound the planner must beat |
| **Rule-Planner, depth + roll couple** | **0.69** | the arm, with level 2 |
| Rule-Planner, depth | 0.60 | the arm |
| *Rule-Planner, optimistic perception* | *0.57* | *perception control, not a result* |
| TROT fixed † | 0.29 | |
| TURN fixed † | 0.17 | |

† the single-skill arms read no terrain at all; their rows carry `perception=n/a`.

**Read the floor first.**  Goal 0 sits **0.50 m from the spawn in 18 of the 20 courses**, so
a score of 1.0 means the robot moved half a metre.  The benchmark's usable range for these
arms is about 0 to 1, not 0 to 8.

## 2. The result that decides the direction: no rule can win

Per cell, the best of the three single-skill arms, averaged with equal weight:

**Oracle 0.81 against WALK-alone 0.76.  A perfect chooser gains 0.06.**

Some skill beats WALK in only **12 of 200 cells** — TROT in 7, TURN in 5.  In the other 188
the best thing the library can do is what WALK already does unaided.

So the planner's deficit is not a rule-quality problem.  **The rules are choosing among
options that are not there.**  Even a planner with a perfect oracle and no switching cost
scores 0.81 against the teacher's 4.83.

## 3. Perception is not the limit either

| | score |
|---|---|
| planner, ground truth through a sensor model (optimistic) | 0.57 |
| planner, rendered depth — occluded, noisy, shaken by the body's own pitch | **0.60** |
| **WALK fixed, which reads nothing at all** | **0.76** |

The real sensor is **not worse** than perfect knowledge, and both are worse than not
looking.  The depth arm's small advantage has a visible mechanism: it **never selects
TROT** (0 cells against the optimistic arm's 14), and TROT fixed scores 0.29 and survives
1 cell of 200.

Depth-arm sanity, printed by the run: 79.6 % of pixels return inside the 2 m far clip, map
coverage 28.8 % of the 41 × 81 local grid, observed heights −1.00 m (ray-carve = a pit) to
+0.16 m.  The camera is eurekaverse's own, read out of `CustomDepthCfg` field by field.

## 4. Where the planner loses to WALK: all of it is TURN

Paired per cell, planner worse in **37**, better in 1, tied in 162.

* **All 37 used TURN.  Not one was lost while running WALK alone.**
* `frac_TURN` median **0.472** in those cells against 0.256 over the whole grid.
* The planner is upright for a median **3.42 s** and spends **1.52 s of it turning on the
  spot at 0.0075 m/s**.
* It travels 0.31 m less than WALK and scores 0.19 against WALK's 1.22.

Same mechanism as the six losing cells on the old terrain, six times as many of them: the
rougher ground turns the heading over faster, the planner re-aims more often, and each
re-aim is most of the life it has left.

## 5. What the terrain change did, and which part of it

Switching from `data/benchmark_frozen.npz` to `legged_eval` added eurekaverse's own
roughness (amplitude 0.02–0.04 m on a 0.075 m grid) and its 0.1 m rim raised 0.5 m, and
moved the terrain draw onto the run seed.

Ablation, WALK, all 200 cells:

| | rim off | rim on |
|---|---|---|
| **roughness off** | 1.09 | 1.09 |
| **roughness on** | 0.76 | **0.76** |

*(the frozen archive — neither, on a different draw — is 1.11)*

**The roughness is the entire drop.**  The rim changes the score *identically*, not
approximately; the different draw is worth 0.02.

Two predictions made in advance failed, for one shared reason.  The ten pit courses were
expected to zero out half the grid: they score **higher** than the non-pit ones (WALK 0.84
vs 0.67).  The rim was expected to help, lane departure having been the main failure: it
does nothing.  **Both because the median robot travels 0.79 m** — pits, rims and the draw
are all properties of ground it never stands on.  Roughness is the only one under it at
the spawn.

## 6. Two protocols, side by side — **do not average these**

| arm | frozen archive (no roughness, no rim) | legged_eval |
|---|---|---|
| Rule-Planner | 1.07 | 0.57 (optimistic) / 0.60 (depth) |
| WALK fixed | 1.11 | 0.76 |
| TROT fixed | 0.97 | 0.29 |

Of 200 cells only **20** are the same terrain in both, and eight courses differ by over a
metre in peak height.  Every results row now carries a `terrain` column so the two can
never be mixed.  The frozen archive is kept — every pre-2026-09-01 number came off it — and
is reachable only through `--terrain frozen`.

**And `4.48` is not a number from this protocol.**  It is eurekaverse's own `evaluate.py`
on teacher `_10_3` (RUN_RESULTS.md, 2026-07-10).  legged_eval's own teacher results read
**4.83 / 4.98** for a different checkpoint.  The scoring rule agrees; the terrain draw does
not — upstream's is unseeded.

## 7. Level 2 — the roll couple

Every termination on this grid is a **roll** termination: 165 of 200 with roughness, 125
without, and **not one pitch termination in 400 episodes** (peak |pitch| median 15°, cutoff
86°).  So the term regulates roll only.

`sim/attitude.py` adds uniform feed-forward hip torque to the legs the recording has on the
ground.  It is the exact dual of the yaw couple on the same joint: uniform *c* gives roll
and no yaw, `c·sign(x)` gives yaw and no roll.

| | off | on | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| WALK, seed 1 | 0.76 | 0.83 | +0.08 |
| WALK, seed 2 | 0.77 | 0.82 | +0.06 |
| WALK, seed 3 | 0.69 | 0.81 | +0.12 |
| **Rule-Planner on depth** | **0.60** | **0.69** | **+0.09** |
| TROT (control) | 0.29 | 0.34 | +0.05, but **−32 % forward speed** |
| TURN (control) | 0.17 | **0.14** | **−0.04** |

Sign settled by measurement: +1 gives 0.83, −1 gives **0.66**, i.e. worse than off.
Forward speed moves −6 / −5 / −1 % across seeds, inside the ±10 % the falsification test
allows.  At gain 16 / cap 4 it scores higher (0.85) and costs **−19 %** of forward speed,
so the recommended operating point is deliberately not the highest-scoring one.
**WALK-only by table** — TROT buys survival by slowing down, TURN is worse.

The design predicted ≈0.98; the delivered 0.82 is about half.  The estimate assumed a
balance term recovers the 44 cells that stop surviving when roughness is added; it recovers
6 to 18 of them.  The rest are not falls a lateral hip force can catch.

## 8. The two courses nothing scores on

`staircase_walking_full_width` and `staircase_spiral` are 0.00 in **every** arm, including
the oracle.  The **teacher scores 4.49 and 4.87** there — the second is above its own
average — so the terrain is not the problem.

They are the only two courses that do not put goal 0 at 0.50 m:

* `staircase_walking_full_width`: goal 0 is at **1.00 m**, sitting **on top of the first
  riser**, which is 0.06 m at difficulty 0 and 0.16 m at difficulty 9 — at or above WALK's
  0.04–0.06 m step limit at *every* difficulty.  The robots stop at 0.75 m and never even
  reach it, so both barriers are real and the near one binds.
* `staircase_spiral`: goal 0 is **2.5–2.7 m ahead and 0.30 m to the right**, over ground
  that is **perfectly flat the whole way**.  Three of ten cells get far enough in x; all
  three miss laterally (they end 0.5–0.8 m to the left of a goal on the right).

**Not a structural limit** — the same two limits everything else has (travel distance,
cross-track accuracy, step height), measured against a goal placed differently.  What it
does show is how much work the 0.50 m placement is doing for the other eighteen scores.

## 10. RUN and JUMP — closed as unresolved, with reasons

* **RUN**: the clip has **no ballistic phase in replay at all**.  Swing gate, loop seam and
  entry phase were each excluded separately.  It collapses in 1.13–2.47 s.
  `run_collapse.md`, `unmeasurable.md`.
* **JUMP**: the recording used **1.10–1.20× the actuator's effort limit** — calf 54.67 N·m
  against 45.43, thigh 26.15 against 23.70 — in bursts of 11–23 ms.  Investigated further
  this week: **23.70 / 45.43 are Unitree's published *maxima*, not ratings.**  The GO motor
  page says "Maximum Torque: 23.7NM" and the Go2 page "Peak Joint Torque: Approx. 45N.m";
  **no continuous rating is published**, so there is no headroom underneath for a peak to
  be 1.5–3× of.  The USD carries no limit at all (PhysX reads 1e9); the clip is only in
  `IdealPDActuatorCfg`.  **No Isaac Lab actuator permits a momentary overshoot** — all
  seven end in a static `_clip_effort`, and `DCMotor`'s dual limit is peak-vs-*speed*, not
  peak-vs-time.  Raising it is a decision that re-scores the E2E arm too, and it is the
  user's; **nothing has been changed.**  `jump_torque.md`.
  * And it would not open the pit courses: `front_jump` travels **26 ± 4 mm** horizontally
    against gaps of 0.10 to 5.15 m.  More torque buys apex, and apex is not what binds.

## 11. Every intervention, and how much each is worth

Full register with values, defaults and costs: `outputs/interventions.md`.  The ones the
current numbers rest on:

| | what | authority | worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1–A7** | plant corrections (202 Hz physics, μ = 1.3, upstream actuator, measured contact, imported mesh, per-step metrics, measured initial condition) | not optional, not flags | the difference between a simulator and a wrong one.  A1 alone was the difference between belly-down at 1.40 s and walking |
| **B1** | foot placement, Raibert lateral | ±0.05 rad cap = **15 mm** of foot travel; 0.03 rad = **9 mm** | the largest single added control; swing legs only, loaded legs keep the recording exactly |
| **B2** | heading hold `--heading heading-only` | ±0.04 rad WALK, ±0.02 TROT | WALK curvature 13.26 → **0.24 °/m**.  Does **not** move this benchmark — at 1.1 m of travel nothing gets far enough for curvature to matter |
| **B/yaw** | stance-leg yaw couple | gain 5 N·m/rad, cap 2.0, peak 11.95 of 23.70, **0 of 2000 steps clipped** | TROT curvature 3.20 → 0.01 °/m; 20 °/s of authority against foot placement's 3.1 |
| **E4** | roll couple (new) | gain 8, cap **2.0 N·m** of 23.70, at the cap on 14 % of stance-leg-steps | WALK 0.73 → 0.82, arm 0.60 → 0.69 |

Every one of B and E is **off by default, banner-printed, stamped into every row, and
exactly null at its zero setting.**

## 12. Harness defects — the ones that cost results

Full list: `outputs/harness_findings.md` (17 entries).  The ones a reader should know:

* **§17 — under Kit, a Python error exits with status 0, no message and no traceback.**
  This is the worst one and it recurs.  A `NameError`, a `SystemExit` and an
  `UnboundLocalError` have each cost a bisection; it cost one again this week when the
  depth camera read `PLANNER_ARM` before it was assigned.  An excepthook is installed and
  it does not always fire.  **Any silent early exit is this until proven otherwise.**
* **§15 — an 11.3 M-triangle terrain cooks without error and collides with nothing.**  All
  200 robots fell through and every cell scored 0, with no error anywhere.  Fixed by one
  collider per task.
* **§5 — PhysX integrated at the clip rate instead of the config rate**, and *lied about
  its cause*: it reported "stride +517 %", "walks backward", "wrong DOF mapping".
* **§6 — every base-derived metric was the final step's value, repeated.**  Invalidated a
  full day.
* **§11 — the traces store quaternions scalar-LAST and an analysis read scalar-first.**
  A yaw of 55° was reported as a roll of 55°.  Undetectable at rest.
* **§12 — the grid's cells are not replicates**: TROT turns 0.3 mm of initial difference
  into 1 m of outcome.
* **A5 — `terrain_type="plane"` lays a 2000 km floor under an imported mesh.**  Produced
  "60/60 reached, 0 % fell" over a trench, and `FOOT_SPAN_X = 0.550 m`, which must not be
  used.  The benchmark now verifies the plane's *absence* and refuses to start otherwise.
* **New this week**: the end-of-run report for the roll couple read the wrong objects and
  printed "0 stance-leg-steps driven" for a term that was working.  Scores unaffected.
  Same shape as CLAUDE.md §6.5 — a plausible number about the wrong quantity.

## 13. What this says to do next

1. **The library is the whole question.**  0.81 is a perfect chooser; 4.83 is the teacher.
   Nothing in the planner closes that.  Two concrete gaps: a gait that holds on 40 mm of
   noise, and a forward-travelling jump.
2. Level 2 is built and is worth +0.09 on the arm.  Its ceiling is measured, not guessed.
3. TURN costs more than it buys in its present form (§4) — 1.5 s of a 3.4 s life at
   0.0075 m/s.  Either a faster re-aim, or cross-track correction inside WALK.
4. `effort_limit` is the user's decision; the evidence is closed (§10).
