# Offline planner sweep — 7200 runs

Generated by `scripts/simulate_planner_offline.py` on the frozen benchmark
(`height_fields_before_fix`, 20 tasks × 10 levels), swept over
`SWITCH_DELAY` ∈ {0, 0.21, 0.45, 0.6, 0.8, 1, 1.4, 2, 2.4} s
× `STEP_WALK_MAX` ∈ {0.06, 0.10, 0.15} m.
Sections 1–4 use the reference `STEP_WALK_MAX` = 0.10 m; §5 varies it.

> **This is not an evaluation.** A kinematic sweep advances the robot at each
> skill's measured steady speed and never decides whether it would have stayed
> upright. What it produces is the **skill sequence** and the **switch count**,
> and how both respond to the switch delay. Success rates need the simulator.

> **12 parameters are `CALIBRATION_NEEDED` placeholders** (§6). §5 exists
> precisely because one of them, `STEP_WALK_MAX`, could otherwise be mistaken for
> the cause of a result that is really the sensor model's doing.

## 1. Effect of the switch delay

`lookahead = BASE_MARGIN + SWITCH_DELAY × speed`, at trot speed
(0.55 m/s). The decision window is
`[max(0.25, lookahead), min(2.00, lookahead + 1.0)]`.
**px width** is the ground covered by one depth pixel *at the commitment point* —
the number that decides whether an obstacle survives the sensor model.
**true / seen** are the largest step in the window before and after the pixel blur,
averaged over the 200 runs at that delay.

## 2. Where perception collapses

Several thresholds get called "the sensor limit". They are not the same number,
and the binding one is not the obvious one:

| boundary | delay | what happens |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| delay is **invisible** | ≤ 0.36 s | required lookahead is still inside the 0.25 m blind zone, so the decision window is unchanged. `(SENSOR_NEAR − BASE_MARGIN) / speed` |
| **blur starts to bite** | **0.8 s** | first swept delay losing ≥ 2 % of the true step height |
| **blur is material** | not reached in this sweep | first swept delay losing ≥ 25 % |
| **half the geometry is gone** | not reached in this sweep | first swept delay losing ≥ 50 % |
| **jump becomes unaimable** | **1.4 s** | first swept delay at which no jump fires at any threshold (§5) |
| past the **reliable range** | > 1.73 s | commitment point beyond 1.0 m |
| fully **blind** | > 3.55 s | commitment point past `far_clip`; the window collapses and the planner reports it |

The binding limit for *this* planner is **1.4 s** — not the
3.55 s at which the window finally goes blind, and (per §6) not a
limit of the sensor either.
Perception does not fail when the commitment point leaves the sensor range. Two
things break long before that, and they break at different delays:

* The **jump stops firing** at **1.4 s** — a hard edge
  rather than a slope, because the near-edge gate is a boolean: `front_jump`
  covers 26 mm of ground, and the gate refuses it the moment the commitment point
  leaves the blind-zone edge. §6 shows this edge is a property of the **decision
  window**, not of the sensor or the robot — a gate that tracks obstacles across
  the full visible range still fires at every delay in this sweep.
* The **blur** degrades gradually from **0.8 s**
  (3 % of the step height lost) to
  13 % at 2.4 s. The pixel footprint
  goes as roughly `d²`, so it overtakes a 0.1–0.2 m step well inside the 2 m range.

Read together: below ~0.4 s the delay is invisible, between
there and **1.4 s** the planner degrades smoothly, and past
**1.4 s** it loses a whole skill outright while still
believing the terrain is flatter than it is. The blur is a real sensor limit; the
skill loss is not, and §6 recovers it.

**Fewer switches at a long delay is not an improvement.** Going from 0 s to 2.4 s drops the mean switch count from 9.2 to 5.8, and the share of time in WALK from 66 % to 58 % — the planner becomes *less* conservative, not more. At 2.4 s the commitment point sits at 1.37 m, where one depth pixel covers 11 cm of ground, so the largest step in the window is box-averaged from 0.23 m down to 0.20 m — below the thresholds that would have triggered a downgrade. Jump triggers go from 127 to 0 over the same range, but for a *different* reason -- see §5, where the counters show the aimability gate shutting well before the blur would have suppressed them. A quieter skill sequence here means the planner stopped seeing the obstacles, not that it handled them better.

## 5. Is the jump count a threshold effect or a resolution effect?

`STEP_WALK_MAX` is a `CALIBRATION_NEEDED` placeholder, and JUMP only fires for a
step in `(STEP_WALK_MAX, STEP_JUMP_MAX]`. Lowering it widens that band, so if the
jumps that disappear at long delay were a threshold artefact, a lower threshold
would bring them back. The three counters separate the possible causes:

* **ticks in band** — the observed step landed inside the jump band at all. Zero
  here means the geometry never even offered a jump: a *resolution* effect,
  because the band's own position moves with `STEP_WALK_MAX` and this column does
  not recover when it is lowered.
* **blocked: window not near** — the step was in the band, but the decision
  window no longer starts at the blind-zone edge. `front_jump` covers 26 mm of
  ground, so it is only aimable at something the robot already stands at; once
  the delay pushes the commitment point outwards this gate shuts. **Structural —
  no threshold value re-opens it.**
* **blocked: retrigger** — in band, window near, but a previous jump has not yet
  been cleared by `JUMP_RETRIGGER_M` of travel.

> **0.15 m closes the band, it does not narrow it.** JUMP fires on `(STEP_WALK_MAX, STEP_JUMP_MAX]` and `STEP_JUMP_MAX` is 0.15 m, so at those rows the interval is empty and the ticks-in-band column is 0 by construction at *every* delay — that is arithmetic, not a measurement about the terrain or the sensor. Both are `CALIBRATION_NEEDED` placeholders; the ordering `STEP_WALK_MAX < STEP_JUMP_MAX` is a structural requirement of the rule, and a calibration that violates it removes the skill.

**Neither — it is a structural gate.** At 2.4 s the observed step still lands in the jump band on 4747 ticks (so the band is reachable, and lowering `STEP_WALK_MAX` to 0.06 m does not change that), but 4747 of those — 100 % — are refused because the decision window no longer starts at the blind-zone edge. `front_jump` covers 26 mm of ground; once the commitment point moves out to 1.37 m there is nothing for it to aim at. **No value of `STEP_WALK_MAX` re-opens that gate**, which is why the jump count is 0 at every threshold tried (0.06, 0.10, 0.15 m). Whether that gate is a real limit or an artefact of how the decision window is shaped is the question §6 answers.

The resolution effect is real but secondary here: the same runs see 0.199 m where the true step is 0.230 m (13 % lost to the pixel footprint), which is what suppresses the *downgrades* in §1. For the jump specifically, the aimability gate shuts first.

Pooled over thresholds, the jump count reaches zero at **1.4 s** and stays there for every longer delay.

## 6. Is the JUMP gate a physical limit or a window artefact?

`front_jump` covers 26 mm of ground, so it has to be launched while the robot is
standing at the step - the command must go out `speed x SWITCH_DELAY` metres early.
Section 5 showed the jump count going to zero at long delay for reasons that are neither
the threshold nor the blur, which leaves two possibilities:

* **physical** - at that delay the decision genuinely has to be made before the obstacle
  can be seen; or
* **a window artefact** - the obstacle *is* visible, but the decision window
  `[lookahead, lookahead + 1.0]` slides away from the robot as the delay grows, so
  the thing it is about to reach falls out of the window's near side and the near-edge
  gate refuses it.

`JumpGate.TRACKING` separates them. It watches the **whole visible range**
(0.25-2.00 m) every tick, converts
each detected step into a world-frame position, and fires when that obstacle's estimated
time of arrival falls to `SWITCH_DELAY`. Detection runs on the **blurred** profile inside
the same range limits - the sensor model is untouched. Only *which part of what the camera
already sees* the planner may act on changes; thresholds, hysteresis, hold time and the
one-command channel are identical.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 127 | 329 | 36169 | 0 | 0 | 10.4 |
| 0.21 | 120 | 326 | 35916 | 0 | 0 | 10.0 |
| 0.45 | 106 | 322 | 35905 | 124 | 1 | 9.4 |
| 0.6 | 98 | 357 | 35137 | 240 | 2 | 9.5 |
| 0.8 | 90 | 346 | 34538 | 332 | 2 | 9.1 |
| 1 | 85 | 325 | 34209 | 379 | 4 | 8.5 |
| 1.4 | 0 | 323 | 33393 | 2526 | 6 | 8.4 |
| 2 | 0 | 300 | 32066 | 2213 | 14 | 7.9 |
| 2.4 | 0 | 315 | 30889 | 1966 | 20 | 7.0 |

**Window design, not a physical limit.** The near-edge gate reaches zero at **1.4 s**. Tracking, on exactly the same sensor model and the same thresholds, still fires 323 jumps there and never reaches zero across the swept range. The obstacles were visible the whole time; the near-edge gate was simply not allowed to look at them, because the decision window slides away from the robot as the delay grows and the thing it is about to reach falls out of the window's near side. **Recorded as a window-design problem, not a capability limit.**

The geometric ceiling on tracking is `SENSOR_FAR / speed` = **3.64 s** at trot
speed: past that the commitment point is further away than the camera can see, and no
amount of tracking helps. That, not the near-edge gate, is where the physical limit sits.

**A caveat on the tracking gate.** It fires on a time-to-arrival computed from the
*current* skill's speed. If the planner changes gait while the jump is in flight the robot
arrives early or late; nothing here measures landing accuracy, and a kinematic sweep
cannot. Tracking restoring the jump count means the decision was *available*, not that the
jump would have landed on the step.
