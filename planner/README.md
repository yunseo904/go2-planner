# planner

Rule-based discrete-skill planner for the Go2 parkour benchmark. CPU only —
nothing here imports Isaac Lab, Isaac Sim or torch (checked in CI-able form by
`grep -rE '^\s*(import|from)\s+(torch|isaac|omni|legged_gym)' planner/`).

```
scripts/simulate_planner_offline.py   frozen terrain × SWITCH_DELAY sweep
                                      → outputs/planner_offline_summary.csv
                                      → outputs/planner_offline_segments.csv
                                      → outputs/planner_offline_unsupported.csv
                                      → outputs/planner_offline.md
```

| module | what it does |
| :--- | :--- |
| `config.py` | every parameter, each tagged `MEASURED` / `DERIVED` / `CALIBRATION_NEEDED` / `CONVENTION` with its source. `DEFAULT.report()` prints the table, `DEFAULT.needs_calibration()` lists what is still open, `DEFAULT.replace(**{"switch.SWITCH_DELAY": 2.4})` makes a variant |
| `features.py` | geometry of the decision window as the depth camera would see it: blind zone, 2 m range cut, pixel-footprint blur, per-range confidence, and a `FeatureMemory` that carries the last valid observation across the blind zone |
| — | steps/slope/width follow the nearest walkable surface within ±0.5 m of the centre line; **gaps do not** — they are read straight off the raw height field (see below) |
| `skills.py` | the four skills (WALK / TROT / RUN / JUMP) built from `config`, plus the `SkillPolicy` interface and two stand-ins: `StandStillPolicy` (wiring test) and `KinematicPolicy` (offline sweeps) |
| `rules.py` | the rule engine: hysteresis, minimum hold time, transition safety conditions, the one-command channel, the one-shot jump, and the `Unsupported` log |
| `tracking.py` | the alternative jump gate: world-frame obstacle tracks over the full visible range, fired on time-to-arrival. Exists to test whether the near-edge gate is a real limit |

## The three things that make this honest

**1. Thresholds are not tuned on scores.** `CLAUDE.md` §2 forbids it. Every
parameter carries a provenance tag, and ten of them are `CALIBRATION_NEEDED`
placeholders — the walking/trotting step limits, the roughness and slope bands,
the foot span. They are wired through `config.py` rather than inlined so a
calibration run replaces them without touching any logic; the probe terrains that
settle five of them are generated by `scripts/freeze_calibration.py` and mapped in
`outputs/calibration_plan.md`. The offline sweep
prints them as a warning before it runs. The *shape* of its result does not
depend on their values; the absolute thresholds do, and are not findings.

**2. The planner only sees what the camera sees.** Handing it the frozen height
field directly would give the rule planner information the E2E policy does not
have. Instead: nothing inside 0.25 m, nothing past 2.0 m, and the followed
surface box-averaged over the ground footprint of one pixel at its range
(~2.7 cm at 0.7 m, ~24 cm at 2.0 m, interpolated as a power law through the two
anchors in `CLAUDE.md` §3).

**3. Terrain with no matching skill is attempted, not skipped.** A gap wider than
`GAP_MAX` (measured: 0.0 m — no skill in the library launches with forward
velocity) or a corridor narrower than the body is logged as an `Unsupported`
event with a reason and a position, and the planner carries on with WALK. All 20
tasks run identically; "tried and failed" is the result.

## The decision window

`lookahead = BASE_MARGIN + SWITCH_DELAY × speed` is the *commitment* distance:
terrain nearer than that will be underfoot before a switch ordered now could take
effect. The window a decision is made on is

    from = max(SENSOR_NEAR, lookahead)
    to   = min(SENSOR_FAR,  lookahead + DECISION_WINDOW_M)

so a longer delay does not blind the camera — it slides the actionable band
outwards into the blurrier part of the range, and past `far_clip` the window
collapses and the planner says so instead of returning flat ground.

Two consequences fall straight out of the geometry and show up in the sweep:

* Any delay below `SENSOR_NEAR / speed` ≈ 0.45 s at trot speed is **invisible**:
  the required lookahead is inside the blind zone, so 0.21 s and 0.00 s produce
  almost the same sequence.
* A 2.4 s delay produces *fewer* switches, but only because a 0.65 m step at
  1.37 m is averaged over an ~11 cm pixel footprint and stops looking like a
  step. Quieter is not better here.

## The jump gate is a window artefact, not a limit

The default gate (`JumpGate.NEAR_EDGE`) only jumps when the decision window still
starts at the blind-zone edge, and the jump count therefore hits zero at 1.4 s of
switch delay at every threshold. That looks like a capability limit and is not.

`JumpGate.TRACKING` watches the whole visible range, keeps world-frame tracks of
detected steps, and fires on time-to-arrival. Same sensor model, same thresholds,
same rule machinery — and it fires 300-330 jumps at *every* delay in the sweep,
including 2.4 s where the near-edge gate fires none. The obstacles were visible
the whole time; the near-edge gate was not allowed to look at them, because
`[lookahead, lookahead + DECISION_WINDOW_M]` slides away from the robot as the
delay grows.

The effective limit is therefore **not 1.4 s**. It is `SENSOR_FAR / speed` =
2.0 m / 0.55 m/s = **3.64 s** at trot speed — the delay at which the commitment
point passes out of camera range and there is nothing left to decide on. 1.4 s
was the near-edge gate's window collapsing, not the robot's reach.

(`outputs/planner_offline.md` quotes two nearby numbers and they are not the same
quantity: §6's **3.64 s** is `SENSOR_FAR / speed`, the reach of the tracking gate;
§4's **3.55 s** is `(SENSOR_FAR − BASE_MARGIN) / speed`, the delay at which the
*window* itself collapses, which happens one `BASE_MARGIN` earlier. Both are far
past 1.4 s, which is the point.)

**One command in flight.** `RulePlanner` holds a single pending command; requests
arriving while one is in flight are dropped and counted, never substituted for
it. Letting a later request overwrite the queue is not a cosmetic bug: with a
2.4 s delay a queued JUMP survives ~24 ticks, and overwriting deleted *every*
long-delay jump while leaving the short-delay numbers untouched — which reads
exactly like a physical limit.

## Gaps are measured differently from everything else

Steps, slope and lateral width follow the **nearest walkable surface within
±0.5 m** of the centre line — the rule `terrain_toolkit.profile` uses, and the
right one, because it is where the robot would actually put its feet and because
stepping-stone goals sit inside the pit.

Applied to *gaps* that same rule hides them. A line of stepping stones has a
stone within half a metre of nearly every point, so the corridor search reports
continuous ground where there is a pit. `max_gap` is therefore read straight off
the raw height field along the centre line, with no route correction and no
blurring — a gap is a topology fact about the ground under the path, not a height
measurement. The corridor-following value is kept alongside as `gap_corridor_m`;
the difference between the two is exactly "how far off the straight line is there
something to stand on", which is what distinguishes a stepping-stone field from a
plain pit.

## Known limits

* `KinematicPolicy` advances the body at each skill's measured steady speed and
  nothing else. The offline sweep is a **skill-sequence generator**, not an
  evaluation — it never decides whether the robot would have stayed upright.
* `step_up_true_m` / `step_down_true_m` are the unblurred steps, kept as a
  **diagnostic only** — they measure how much the sensor model removed. The rules
  must never read them; the planner does not get to see the height field.
* `RUN` is slower than `TROT` in the measurements. At `speed_level 0` the running
  trot buys an aerial phase, not ground speed.
